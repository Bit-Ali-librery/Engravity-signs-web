OUR STORY

Born in the workshop.
Building what we always lacked.

Engravity wasn't born in an incubator or an office. It was born in a workshop with MDF dust, router noise, and the frustration of knowing that every workflow we relied on was broken.

We are a woman and a man with more than 25 combined years working in sign workshops. We've cut on CNC, engraved with laser, printed in 3D, sanded by hand, washed parts at 11pm, fought with software from the nineties, and delivered signs that made us proud the next day.

In all that time, one thing never changed: the software didn't serve us. We designed in Illustrator because that's what was there. We validated ADA with rulers and a calculator. We generated G-Code with programs that felt like they were from the nineties. Every time we switched tools, we lost something in translation.

After a couple of decades doing that, we asked ourselves something simple: what if we built the tool we always wanted to have? One that understood the real workflow of a workshop, that didn't treat ADA validation as an afterthought, that generated clean G-Code without needing three intermediate exports.

That's Engravity. And we built it with the same patience we used cutting our first sign — knowing that the first versions are never perfect, but that every iteration brings the tool closer to the reality of the shop floor.

How we got here

An honest timeline — no varnish, no marketing.

~2000

Early days in the workshop

We started working in sign production without knowing we were going to stay for two decades. We learned by cutting, engraving, and sanding — not in a course.

2010 — 2020

A decade of production

We went through every process: CNC routing, laser cutting, Logojet 3D printing, vinyl cutting, industrial finishing. Each one taught us where the software was falling short.

2023

The idea

We made a list of everything we'd want in an ideal piece of software. It was long. We decided to start with the most painful: automatic ADA validation and G-Code that didn't need post-editing.

2024 — 2025

Building the engine

Two years of quiet development. We validated every feature against real files from projects we had already produced. If the software failed where we had previously struggled, we fixed it before adding anything new.

March 2026

v1.0 to the world

We launched the first public version. The people who try it keep telling us what's missing, what's redundant, and what makes them come back. We are listening.

Today

Learning in public

Engravity is alive and changing every week. We don't have a product committee in a distant room — the priority is what the maker community asks us for.

How we work

These aren't corporate values pinned to a wall — they're the rules we use to make decisions every day.

🪚

Built from the workshop

Every feature responds to a real pain we lived in production, not to a pretty idea in a meeting. If we never suffered it ourselves, we question it.

🤝

Learn in public

We don't pretend to know everything. When we get something wrong we say so, and when someone teaches us something we incorporate it. The community is part of the product team.

🎓

Access, not exclusivity

Professional tools shouldn't be a luxury. That's why the Student plan exists for free — so anyone learning can have the same toolkit as an industrial workshop.

🛠️

Quality without theater

We won't invent features just to sell. If a function doesn't solve a real problem, we leave it out — even if a competitor has it.

📐

Precision over magic

AI is useful when it saves time, but the final file has to be exact. We prefer a predictable workflow over an impressive trick.

🌎

We speak many languages

Engravity was born bilingual. The shops that use it run in English, Spanish, French, and Portuguese. Translation isn't optional — it's part of the product from day one.
