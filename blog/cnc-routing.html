Mastering CNC: Optimal Parameters for Acrylic and Wood

By the Engravity founding team (25 years of experience) Reading time: 4 minutes

After decades in the industry, we see that the main problem isn't the CNC machines themselves but the parameters (feeds and speeds) the software sends to the controller. Cutting acrylic and wood requires completely opposite thermal calculations.

Routing Cast and Extruded Acrylic

The challenge isn't cutting — it's preventing the material from melting and the chips from welding back into the rear cut. If your edge comes out cloudy or rough, the friction (temperature) is too high.

  • Tooling: Solid carbide single-flute "O-flute" bits are mandatory. They maximize chip evacuation, pulling debris away from the central heat.
  • Spindle speed (RPM): Stay between 12,000–18,000 RPM. Going higher almost guarantees the acrylic will melt unless you have very high-pressure forced air.
  • Feed rate: Aim for 75 to 300 inches per minute (IPM). If the chips look like dust, you're going too slow (or the RPM is too high). Look for the "flake-style chip".

Engraving and Routing in Hardwoods

Unlike acrylic which melts, wood suffers from the dreaded "tear-out" (splintering on the top or bottom layer of the material). The game here is cleanliness and surface finish.

  • Tooling: Compression bits. These combine "up-cut" geometry on the lower half and "down-cut" geometry on the upper half. They pull material toward the center, preventing chip-out on both faces of the board.
  • RPM: Wood tolerates high revolutions very well. 18,000 to 24,000 RPM is ideal depending on the hardness (walnut vs. pine).
