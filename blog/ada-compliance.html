The Definitive Compliance Guide: ADA Section 703 (2025)

By the Engravity founding team Reading time: 6 minutes

Under the 2010 federal standards (Standards for Accessible Design), signs that identify fixed rooms and spaces must meet rigorous technical requirements. At Engravity, we've documented that more than 40% of signs produced in the traditional industry get rejected for failing these technical minimums. Here's what you need to know before machining:

1. Tactile character specifications (letters)

  • Strict dimensions: tactile letter height must never be less than 5/8 inch (0.625") nor greater than 2 inches (2.0"). Width-to-height proportions are also regulated.
  • Depth: raised text must protrude at least 1/32 inch (0.0312") above the surrounding plate.
  • Font & case: all tactile lettering must use a sans-serif font and stay 100% in UPPERCASE — no italics.

2. Non-negotiable Braille requirements

  • Braille type: the alphabet alone is NOT enough — the law requires Contracted (Grade 2) Braille. Our software translates and domes it for you, so you don't have to learn the combinations.
  • Dot shape (domed): cutting perfect cylinders with a laser violates the rule — the dots must be domed (rounded) on top to be perceptible to the touch.
  • Positioning: Braille must always sit directly below the raised text — never to its sides — with a 3/8 inch (0.375") clear zone around it to avoid tactile confusion.

3. Wall installation

Even the most perfect sign will fail an inspection if it's placed wrong. The golden rule is to measure from the floor: the base of the lowest tactile character must sit no lower than 48 inches, and the top of the highest character no higher than 60 inches. Always mount it on the latch side of the door so it can be read while the door is open.

At Engravity, the entire Section 703 is encoded inside the vector engine itself. When you design with us, the CNC won't export a sign an inspector could reject. Compliance is baked into your G-Code.
