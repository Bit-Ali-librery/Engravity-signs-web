The Definitive Compliance Guide: ADA Section 703 (2025)

By the Engravity founding team Reading time: 6 minutes

Under the 2010 federal standards (Standards for Accessible Design), signs that identify fixed rooms and spaces must meet rigorous technical requirements. At Engravity, we've documented that more than 40% of signs manufactured in the traditional industry get rejected for failing these technical minimums. Here's what you need to know when machining:

1. Tactile Character Specifications (Letters)

  • Strict dimensions: Tactile letter height must never be less than 5/8 inch (0.625") nor greater than 2 inches (2.0"). Width-to-height proportions are also regulated.
  • Depth: Raised text must protrude exactly a minimum of 1/32 inch (0.0312") above the surrounding plate background.
  • Font & case: All tactile lettering must mandatorily use sans-serif fonts and remain 100% in UPPERCASE — no italics.

2. Absolute Braille Requirements

  • Braille type: The alphabet alone is NOT enough — the law requires Contracted (Grade 2) Braille. Our software translates and domes it instantly without requiring you to learn the combinations.
  • Dot shape (domed): Cutting perfect cylinders with a laser is illegal — the dots must be domed (rounded) on top to be perceptible.
  • Positioning: Braille text must always sit below the raised text, never to its sides, with a perimeter clear zone of 3/8 inch (0.375") to avoid tactile confusion.

3. Wall Installation

Even the most perfect sign will be fined if it's placed incorrectly. The golden rule is to measure from the floor: the base of the lowest letter cannot drop below 48 inches, and the maximum letter height cannot exceed 60 inches. Always located on the latch side of the door so it can be read while the door is open.

At Engravity, we encode the entire Section 703 inside the Vector Algorithm itself. When you design with us, the CNC will not export a sign that an inspector would reject. Structural prevention is built into your G-Code.
